# Talk turns and session stories (brainstorm)

The loop has two moves. A **question batch** asks the person to react or to choose; it stays the usual move. A **talk turn** explains in chat, and the person replies in words. A **session story** tells the person what one sitting did. A decision the person makes on ground the person has not seen teaches nothing, and it does not hold.

## When to talk
Take a talk turn in place of the next batch when:
- research, a bounded read, or a coherence pass returns something the person has not seen;
- the person asks a question, or asks you to explain, discuss, talk about, or go deeper into something, also in the free text of an answer;
- a new area turns on specialist knowledge that the person has not shown, for example a statistical test, a rule of the sport, or a licence;
- the person picks every option on ground that is new to the person, or two answers contradict each other.

Take no two talk turns in a row unless the person asks for more. Everything else stays a batch.

## A talk turn
1. Write in chat, in plain words and short paragraphs, about 150 to 350 words. Say:
   - what the thing is and how it works, with one concrete example from this product;
   - what the research, the code, or the data showed, with its source;
   - what it changes on the board: the decisions it supports, and the decisions it puts in question;
   - your own view, and your reason.
2. End the turn with one plain sentence that invites a reply: what the person thinks, what to explain more, or "go on" for the questions. Ask no question form in the same turn. The host can hide the chat text before a question form; a turn that ends in prose is never hidden.
3. Record what the talk explained as findings or ideas, and log one entry with kind `talk`.
4. Read the reply as an answer (2.2). The first batch after a talk turn names what the talk explained in one line, not again in full. In each choice question, give your view and its reason in the question text.

## A session story
Tell the story of a sitting:
- when the person says `pause`, or chooses to stop at a check-in;
- as the narrative lead of the chat return (Step 4);
- on a resume, before the first batch: the story of the last session. When that session has no story, because the person left without a pause, write it from that session's log entries first.

A story is plain prose of about 100 to 200 words, not a list of decisions. It has five parts:
1. What we set out to explore in this sitting.
2. What we learned: the two or three things that change how the person thinks, each as a statement.
3. What we decided, and why: the few decisions that matter most, not every decision.
4. What is still open.
5. Where the next sitting starts.

Write no count of items, questions, or areas, and name no mode mechanism. *Weak:* "A coherence pass settled two conflicts." *Better:* "Two earlier decisions disagreed about the time a verdict may take, and you chose a quick preview, with the blocking verdict an hour later."

Record the story in the board's `stories` list with its session number, add it at the top of the document's `## Sessions` section, and republish the page (2.9). After a `pause`, write both files and return per Step 4. The loop continues on the resume.
