# Persuade and Experience (`design/mode-persuade.md`)

Parts adapted from impeccable v4.7.0 (`pbakaus/impeccable`, `374a0e179`), Apache-2.0.

The design mode reference for two kinds of surface ([_design-context.md](_design-context.md) → Design modes):
- **Persuade.** The person decides and acts; the design is the product. Landing pages, marketing, campaigns, pricing.
- **Experience.** The person is inside the work itself. Portfolios, galleries, showcases.

On these surfaces the design is what the visitor judges, so the world of the direction may own and structure the page. The surfaces span every genre: tech, luxury, consumer products, creative studios. They share the stance (communicate, not transact) and diverge in aesthetic. Do not collapse them into one visual lane.

Design stage Step 3b reads `## Directions`. Step 4 reads `## Screens`.

## Directions

**The slop test.** A visitor asks "how was this made?", not "which tool made this?". Restraint without intent reads as mediocre, not refined. A Persuade or Experience surface needs a point of view, a specific audience, and a willingness to risk strangeness. Apply the anti-convergence rules of [_design-context.md](_design-context.md) → Shared design laws.

**Name the lane.** Before you commit to a direction, name the reference it starts from. A type-specimen page is one lane; a quiet product minimalism is another; acid maximalism is another. Do not drift into editorial-magazine aesthetics on a brief that is not editorial.

**What the opening does.**
- **Persuade.** The opening makes the offer clear and desirable, shows a clear action, and shows something only this product can prove. The action is the one the category's visitors came to take, in its working form: a route-and-dates search for an airline, open slots for a clinic, add to cart for a store. A decorative link to it elsewhere does not count. A hook lands in one line; the primary action is visible; the reading order is clear. A committed form that hides the offer or the action is not finished.
- **Experience.** The work itself leads from the first viewport. The interface recedes.

**Interface languages.** When the source of a direction is itself an interface language, the surface may carry its grammar across navigation, content, controls and states, within the conventions of the platform.

**Typography.** Find the face for the brand as a physical object: a museum caption, a 1970s terminal manual, a fabric label. Reject the first face that "looks designy". The font reflex list is in [typeset.md](typeset.md) → Font selection. Use the Persuade type scale of [_design-context.md](_design-context.md) → Shared design laws; a flat scale reads as uncommitted. Display type may carry the voice.

**Color.** Committed or higher is the default. [_design-context.md](_design-context.md) → Shared design laws defines each strategy. An unexpected combination is allowed when the strategy calls for it.

**Layout.** Asymmetry is available: editorial grids, white space as content. Several columns are a design choice, not a grid default. The page may tell a story as the visitor scrolls.

**Motion.** Orchestrated entrances, staggered reveals and scroll-driven motion are available when they serve the page. One rehearsed entrance beats scattered micro-interactions. Match motion to the personality: a playful brand may carry a spring with bounce 0.1–0.3; an austere brand stays restrained and slow. Motion stays interruptible and inside the frame budget ([animate.md](animate.md)).

## Screens

**The world carries the page; it is not a picture of the page.** A facade, a shutter or a book can be what the page is built on. A viewport painted whole as a facade, a book cover or a poster, with no working parts, is a picture of the world, not a website.
- Cover the page with the world's material: its material is the ground and the major surfaces, and its type sets the headlines. A world reduced to an accent on a neutral template is the category default with a sticker on it.
- Coverage is not performance. One region acts out the concept; the rest stay in the world's material without performing it.
- Two results erase the direction: the painted poster, and the generic product page (a headline over subcopy and a button, a row of equal cards below). A split hero with a badge row, or a centered headline over a row of cards, is the template whatever world paints it.

**The parts still work.** Wherever the world puts a part, the page works as a page:
- navigation set into the world reads as links;
- copy reads as set text, against whatever sits behind it;
- the primary action is clearly clickable and unmistakable;
- the sections keep a steady vertical rhythm, and the next section starts as a section.

Fix a poster by making its parts work where the world put them. Do not move them into a standard layout on the world's color.

**An object stays one object.** When the world is an object, the page shows that one object once, at one scale, from one point of view, in its real form. A book opens to two pages on one gutter, never a third panel. More content goes on the object's own surfaces or on the next spread below, never on extra copies.

**Later screens go further.** The second and third screens of a direction each go further than the first, never cleaner: more of the page's structure in the world's material, a larger or stranger focal move, or a composition the category would not attempt.
