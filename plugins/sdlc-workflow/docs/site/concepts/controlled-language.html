Controlled language

Read this to understand the writing contract behind the plugin's text. All text the workflow writes follows 22 rules distilled from ASD-STE100 (Simplified Technical English), plus one workflow-specific section for story sections and chat summaries. One shared file holds the rules, every writing surface cites that file, and three audit surfaces enforce it.

Why the plugin controls its language

Ambiguous text causes wrong actions. A step that names one command two ways makes the reader guess whether the two names are one command. A pronoun that can point at two nouns makes the reader guess which noun to act on. An instruction hidden inside a note is easy to miss. Each of these failures costs more than it appears to cost, because the reader acts on the guess.

A controlled language removes the guesswork. Each word keeps one meaning. Each instruction gets its own sentence. Each warning comes before the step that it protects. The result is text that means the same thing to every reader — human or agent — on the first read.

Controlled text also helps the agents that run the plugin. The same ambiguity that confuses a human reader causes an agent to misread an instruction. One meaning per word and one instruction per sentence remove that failure mode at the source.

The contract

The contract lives in one file: skills/wf/reference/_ste-procedural.md. It distills Part 1 of ASD-STE100 Issue 9 into 22 rules that a reviewer can check mechanically, and adds one workflow-specific section for story sections and chat summaries. Each distilled rule keeps its ASD-STE100 number for traceability. The file has four sections:

W1–W8
Word discipline. One term per concept, one meaning per term, verbs instead of nominalizations, no phrasal verbs, no contractions, no Latin abbreviations, pronouns with exactly one referent, and concrete conditions instead of abstract statements.
I1–I9
Instruction rules. Imperative mood, one instruction per sentence, sentence caps (20 words procedural, 25 descriptive), condition before command, active voice, simple tenses, vertical lists for sequences, notes that inform and never instruct, and limits that stay with their action.
S1–S5
Warnings and structure. A graded risk word first, the warning before the step that it protects, the consequence stated, one topic per paragraph, and identical wording for identical operations.
A1–A6
Story sections and chat summaries. This section is the workflow's own extension, not from ASD-STE100. It fixes the story heading, three beats in order — the state the stage inherited, the load-bearing decisions with reasons, what the stage enables next plus the top open risk — a one-to-three-paragraph cap, and the compressed chat-summary form.

Where the contract applies

The contract governs all text the workflow writes: every document that /wf docs or the diataxis skill writes, every section of every workflow artifact — the story section included — reader-visible text in view fragments, chat summaries and their receipt fields, product copy, and external-facing outputs such as release notes and PR bodies. Nothing is exempt. Each passage follows the sections that govern its kind of text.

Story sections follow one extra section: the arc. The arc fixes three beats in order — the state the stage inherited, the load-bearing decisions with their reasons, and what the stage enables next plus the top open risk. The language rules apply in full; only the structure is extra. The story section carries the trajectory of the work, and the structured sections beneath it stay terse, so one document reads as one voice.

How the contract is enforced

Three audit surfaces check the same rules from three directions:

  • The ste-compliance review dimension audits text at rest. Every finding cites the rule that it violates (W1–W8, I1–I9, S1–S5, A1–A6) and carries the corrected sentence. Severity follows reader consequence: ambiguity that can change what a reader does is HIGH, and register mechanics are batched LOW.
  • The /wf docs orchestrator audit grades each document against the contract alongside its accuracy, quadrant, completeness, and freshness checks.
  • The ambiguous-copy surface-defect class records the same failures when a live surface shows them — the same feature named differently on two screens, or a dialog whose buttons do not answer the question that the dialog asks.

Provenance

ASD-STE100 is the controlled-language specification that the aerospace industry uses for maintenance documentation. The specification is copyrighted by ASD, so the plugin does not embed it. The contract file paraphrases the transferable rules in the plugin's own words, with examples from the plugin's own domain, and drops the rules that depend on the specification's controlled dictionary.
